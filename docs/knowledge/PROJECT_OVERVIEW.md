# Towards Victory — Project Overview

## Mod Identity

- **Mod Name:** Towards Victory (胜利条件)
- **Mod ID:** `eu5mp.towards_victory`
- **Version:** 0.1.0
- **Target:** EU5 `1.*.*`
- **Status:** In Development — All 6 Victory paths fully implemented: Conquest, Prosperity, Trade, Diplomatic, Cultural, and Scientific
- **Language:** English + Simplified Chinese

Towards Victory adds 6 generalized victory paths to EU5: Conquest, Prosperity, Trade, Diplomatic, Cultural, and Scientific. Each path has 5 milestone nodes that grant permanent buffs when reached. Progress is displayed via situation panels; milestones trigger popup events that notify the player and deliver the reward.

Trade League law update: `data/trade_league_laws.yaml` now drives generated `tv_trade_league_laws.txt`, adding five three-level vote-based laws: Customs Union, Maritime Convoys, Merchant Courts, Staple Monopolies, and Investment Code. Each level-1 policy trades roughly one benefit package for one cost, level 2 gives about 1.5x the same benefits for the same cost, and level 3 gives about 3x the same benefits for two distinct costs. AI policy vote bias uses base weights 200/100/0 for levels 1/2/3; levels 2/3 also scale from opinion of the leader (-50..+50 / -100..+100), leader diplomatic reputation (+2/+5 per point), and leader monthly trade income (+5/+10 per 100 income). Trade League law votes use the Grand Merchant law-change availability trigger and mutually block trade-chain, commercial-intelligence, and monopoly maintenance work.

The **Conquest Victory** path (征服胜利) is fully implemented: 5 triggers, 5 reward modifiers, 5 events, and localization in English and Simplified Chinese. Score = direct locations + 0.5 × subject-or-below locations; thresholds at 200 / 400 / 600 / 1200 / 2500.

The **Prosperity Victory** path (繁荣胜利) is fully implemented: 5 triggers, 5 reward modifiers, 5 events, and localization in English and Simplified Chinese. Score = (total_population + Σdev + country_tax_base) × 50 / (N + 50) × (1 + stability/100) × (gov_power/100), where N = num_owned_locations and gov_power = legitimacy/republican_tradition/devotion/tribal_cohesion/horde_unity by government type. The 50N/(N+50) effective scale term provides stronger diminishing returns on country size. Updated yearly; thresholds at 2,000 / 3,000 / 5,000 / 7,000 / 11,000 (provisional, calibrate after playtesting).

**Prosperity main mechanic:** Prosperity M1 creates the **Engineering Department IO** (`tv_engineering_department`), a non-unique founder-only IO with no parliament tab and no automatic leader election. Its appointed character leader is the **Great Engineer**, stored on the leader country as `tv_great_engineer_char` and managed by generated Appoint/Remove/Change leader actions. Wonder Construction implements **Conception, Debate, Survey, Organization, Logistics, Construction, Ceremony, and Finalization** for 43 universal wonder types, all managed through the same generated mechanics layer from `data/wonders.yaml` and `data/wonder_mechanics.yaml`: Sacred Mountain Complex through Great Monastery, spanning civil production, military defense, civic timekeeping, arts, diplomacy, law, commerce, finance, industry, maritime trade, civilizational memory, prehistoric ritual, cathedral, monastic, and urban building-cap expansion wonders. Appointing a Great Engineer rebuilds the country's feasible proposal deck from owned terrain/city/port/infrastructure/mining conditions, then rolls or restores three simultaneous proposal slots on that engineer. The overview shows three wonder buttons, each labeled by size icon and wonder name; selecting one changes the Great Engineer's short pitch to that wonder's size-aware summary. Accepting locks the currently selected wonder and enters debate, while refuting or bribing refreshes all three proposal slots at once; rejected proposals are removed from the current deck, and if rejection empties the feasible deck the country immediately rebuilds the feasible list and rolls again. Debate uses the Engineering Department IO variable `tv_wonder_domestic_support` (0-200): estate demands for Nobles, Burghers, and Clergy can be accepted for +20 support through `tv_change_wonder_domestic_support_effect`, whose effect localization renders the IO variable change. Refuting or bribing estate demands does not exclude that agenda type. Demand types include the baseline privilege/tax/levy/policy agenda set plus resource-loss demands for 10 Prestige, 7 Stability, 5 Legitimacy, 5% loyalty from the other two estates, 2.5% inflation, one cabinet member, one artist, or one artwork; the cabinet/artist/artwork demands reroll matching slots after execution so two estates cannot keep pointing at a just-consumed scarce target. Monthly debate events can swing support by +/-20, and the debate can end at 100 support. After debate ends, support decays by 1 per month until the wonder is finalized; reaching 0 before finalization automatically declares construction complete if at least one wonder level is complete and the project can enter ceremony, otherwise it forces project collapse. Abandoning or forced collapse applies upper-estate satisfaction penalties, removes the Great Engineer, costs 20 Prestige, and resets wonder project state.

After debate, the player starts a location-select survey through `tv_wonder_start_survey`. Valid sites depend on the locked wonder type: Sacred Mountain uses hills/plateau/mountains; Triumphal Axis uses city/megalopolis; Great Port and Great Lighthouse use ports; Giant Necropolis uses hills or adjacency to city/town; Hydraulic Workshop uses land; Mining City now requires a mineral-producing raw material site (iron, copper, tin, lead, silver, or gold). Fresh survey and construction candidate checks no longer exclude locations that already contain other TV wonder intermediate, module, or final buildings; priority resume/upgrade projects are still allowed only when the saved site matches the currently locked wonder and is either an unfinished intermediate project with no final wonder building or a final wonder building below its stored scale cap. Survey progress is the Engineering Department IO variable `tv_wonder_survey_progress`; each monthly tick adds 0.1 times the Great Engineer's MIL and can fire one survey event from a single random roll. The country tracks the current survey site and three visible competence values: `tv_wonder_scale_competence`, `tv_wonder_logistics_competence`, and `tv_wonder_organization_competence`, initialized at 0 when a new survey begins. At survey start, hidden target competence values are precomputed from 25 base competence, each field's random 0-25 roll, the site preference bonus, and up to 10 from the Great Engineer's ADM; the visible competence values then rise by fixed monthly increments until completion. Monthly survey events can still raise or lower one visible competence by 5, or offer resource tradeoffs for +5. Site preference bonuses use: Sacred Mountain topography/vegetation (mountains +15, plateau +7.5, hills +0, forest +10, woods +5); Triumphal Axis development (0-100 maps linearly to 0-20) plus +5 for megalopolis; Great Port and Great Lighthouse harbor capacity (`harbor_suitability`, 0-100% maps linearly to 0-25, capped above 100%); Giant Necropolis hills +15, adjacent city +10 or adjacent town +5; Hydraulic Workshop total local building levels 0-100 maps linearly to 0-25; Mining City development 0-100 maps linearly to 0-20, plus +5 when the mineral site is a rural settlement. The survey card's Suitability Knowledge section always shows the current wonder's country-scoped build/location trigger through the normal trigger condition list; its suitability rows remain visible as placeholders until surveys reveal them, and revealed rows display actual-vs-maximum preference bonus as `?/+x` before the current site survey completes or `+y/+x` after completion. Each fresh completed survey increments the country variable `tv_wonder_suitability_revealed_<prototype_key>` by one up to that prototype's row count, so unique wonders and their prototype share reveal progress. Completed survey results are stored on the location per wonder type; selecting an already surveyed site restores the stored result instead of restarting progress and does not advance suitability knowledge. Scale competence maps to maximum wonder construction level using thresholds 20/40/60/80/100, with level 6 requiring 100% competence; Organization Competence maps linearly from 0-100% to a 0-20 Wonder Worksite level cap; Logistics Competence maps linearly from 0-100% to a 50%-150% monthly construction progress multiplier. The UI displays these three effects as concise three-line text blocks with the numeric value on the middle line. `tv_wonder_select_construction_site` confirms the completed survey site, grants 0/20/40 domestic support from the average result (<50 / 50-74 / >=75), and unlocks organization, logistics, and construction.

The generated site rules cover all generic wonders: University City excludes rural settlements and favors cities plus local literacy; Sky Dome Grand Temple requires state-religion population and favors monasteries, cathedrals, and higher state-religion share; Giant Tower Temple has no hard site limit and uses the same religious preference profile; River Extension requires a river site and favors development, bridges, and adjacent River Extensions; National Shipyard requires coast and favors harbor suitability plus development; Star Fortress City excludes rural settlements and favors mountains, plateau, forts, and cities; Great Wall excludes cities and favors mountains, plateau, forts, and rural settlements; Giant Armory excludes rural settlements and favors existing armory infrastructure, cities, and development; Library of Nation is capital-only and favors development, cities, and megalopolises.

`data/wonder_mechanics.yaml` is the shared mechanical data source for all 43 generic Engineering Department wonders: it drives final building local effects and maintenance, permanent base modifiers, generic ritual data for the 43 non-unique wonders, generated ritual bridges for unique wonders, proposal deck bridges, site triggers, player-visible `site_rules.*.suitability_knowledge` rows, generated concepts/localization/GUI text, generated ceremony requirement/effect detail panels, and finalization bridges. `buildings.*.final_local` is the shared authored local-effect map for final wonder buildings; it is not keyed by final branch, so the final-building generator applies the same local-effect package to all three generic final branches, and unique final buildings inherit that prototype package through their strict `base_effect_multiplier = 2`. All generic wonders use the same three-style ritual framework: style 1 starts a one-year national burden and later grants a weaker permanent blessing of the same modifier family, style 2 constructs a wonder-specific auxiliary annex building with unique local effects and +50% local estate power for the wonder's estate, and style 3 consumes one fixed decoration cost branch (one artwork, `scaled_gold = 5`, or 50 Prestige) for a large one-time reward. Display-only scripted effects feed the ceremony UI's original-style effect tooltip lists; style-2 annex rewards use generated location static modifiers in `src/main_menu/common/static_modifiers/` so the local modifier package can be shown through `add_location_modifier`. Generic ritual confirmation is split across `tv_wonder_confirm_ceremony`, `tv_wonder_confirm_ceremony_scaled_gold`, and `tv_wonder_confirm_ceremony_prestige`; unique wonders still auto-lock style 1 on construction completion, but that single branch now also goes through the normalized `ritual` schema from `data/unique_wonders.yaml`, where `mode` is the start profile and completion can be driven by configured listeners plus `completion_trigger_script`, optional `snapshot_effect_script` / `progress_effect_script`, and branch-specific runtime variables.

`data/unique_wonders.yaml` defines the unique historical wonder layer (ids 101-150). Each entry maps to an existing generic wonder through `base_key`, inherits the generic size, site rules, suitability knowledge, buildings, and base country effect shape, then applies a 2x base-effect multiplier, a fixed `location`, unique concept/name/flavor/history-intro/image prompt, and one unique ritual definition through either the legacy `ceremony` block or the newer `ritual` block. The normalized unique `ritual` schema now supports `listeners`, `snapshot_effect_script`, `progress_effect_script`, `completion_trigger_script`, and `runtime_variables` in addition to the older start/completion hooks, so a single auto-locked ritual can complete on timer expiry, annex completion, war victory, ruler death, country building thresholds, or "N new buildings since the ritual began" baselines without requiring a multi-branch UI. Great Engineer proposal order is: unfinished resume/expand priority projects first, then feasible unique wonders before the generic random deck; proposal-slot removal still excludes already proposed unique wonders from the current reroll deck. Accepting a unique wonder applies +50 initial domestic support after IO progress reset. Unique survey target competence gains +100 Scale, +20 Logistics, and +20 Organization at survey start. When construction completes, unique wonders auto-select and lock ceremony style 1, so the GUI shows their single ritual text and Hold Ceremony action without the three style-choice buttons; that one branch can now still be authored as an immediate resolution, a timed ritual, an auxiliary ritual building flow, or a deferred event-driven ritual that waits on its configured listeners. `data/wonder_localization.yaml` is the shared source for generated wonder-mechanics localization text. The root-level `wonder_localization_editor_web/` FastAPI tool reads/writes that same localization source and exposes structured mechanics editing for site rules, player-visible suitability knowledge rows, base country modifiers, generic/unique rituals, and final-building local effects; unique pages show inherited prototype site/suitability data and inherited+doubled prototype local effects read-only. The editor reads `data/wonder_editor_catalog.yaml` for precomputed vanilla country/local modifier options whose static modifiers are applied by vanilla events, plus Style 3 instant reward options generated from vanilla event effects. `scripts/wonder_localization_editor.py` remains the compatibility launcher, while hand-maintained wonder-concept and Engineering Department localization files stay as direct-edit side inputs for keys they still own.

Wonder construction currently relies on site-only `tv_wonder_labor_camp` support buildings plus the `tv_wonder_materials` market good. `tv_wonder_labor_camp` is localized as Wonder Worksite, unlocks after Organization/Logistics/Construction, employs 2,000 laborers per level, and each effective level provides 1,000 base monthly construction speed while consuming normal maintenance plus 20 `tv_wonder_materials`. `tv_wonder_materials` uses masonry-like demand properties, `default_market_price = 5`, and market supply, demand, and stockpile represent the material reserve; the Engineering Department panel no longer exposes the Wonder Material Workshop build action while that chain is being refactored. Material depots and the old IO material-stockpile variable are removed. Monthly construction ticks recompute effective worksite levels, set the worksite cap from Organization Competence, and set `tv_wonder_monthly_construction_progress` directly from effective Wonder Worksite speed, so the active unit advances by national effective worksite levels at 1,000 speed per level. A separate construction random-event pulse starts as soon as a construction site is selected (`tv_wonder_stage = 3`), ignores current construction speed, and stops when construction is declared complete, the wonder is finalized, or project state is cleared by abandonment/collapse. That pulse uses one generated `random_list` with 90 no-event weight and a 10-weight nested deck of 212 eligible events: 168 token-exchange events plus 44 Great Engineer events gated by effective MIL thresholds 20/50/80. Construction event localization is now hand-maintained in EN/ZH instead of generated string concatenation; event options still re-check eligibility for stale popups and rely on real visible effects, while IO variable changes use `custom_description` plus effect localization. Each construction unit uses the IO variable `tv_wonder_construction_progress`; small/medium/large wonders require 100,000/200,000/300,000 progress per unit. Completing a unit materializes the corresponding local module building, grants +10 support, and may raise `tv_wonder_level` when all four parts catch up, granting another +10 support plus a level-up notification. Once `tv_wonder_level >= 1`, the player may declare construction complete, which destroys the site Wonder Worksites, locks further construction, awards +20 support, and unlocks ceremonies. All 40 generic wonders now share one generated three-style ritual structure instead of hand-authored per-wonder ceremony logic: style 1 starts a timed national ritual that applies a one-year negative country modifier and, on expiry, grants a weaker permanent blessing of the same modifier family; style 2 starts construction of a wonder-specific ritual annex building whose local modifier package is unique per wonder and whose building effect also adds +50% local estate power for the wonder's associated estate; style 3 is a fixed decoration branch that either destroys one artwork, charges `scaled_gold = 5`, or charges 50 Prestige and then grants the wonder's assigned large one-time reward immediately. Style-1 completion is checked by the monthly ritual pulse, style-2 completion comes from the annex building's `on_built`, and style-3 finalizes immediately after its cost and reward resolve. Unique wonders still expose only one auto-locked ritual branch in the current UI, but that branch now runs through the same normalized ritual execution layer and can therefore resolve as an immediate ritual, a timed ritual checked by the monthly pulse, or an auxiliary ritual building that finalizes on `on_built`; the shared confirmation routing also supports artwork, `scaled_gold = 5`, and 50-Prestige costs on unique rituals when configured. Finalization still triggers a visible inauguration event with unique flavor text per wonder/final building, constructs the matching event-only local wonder building at the selected site, raises that building to the current wonder level, applies the base wonder-level country modifier, applies generated unique ritual country modifiers where configured, grants extra support rewards scaled from remaining Domestic Support (0-200 support to 0-10 Stability, 0-10 Legitimacy, 0-20 Prestige), adds national and local wonder-completion celebration modifiers scaled by completed level, immediately adds +15% site prosperity per completed level, broadcasts a neutral no-effect world-news event with wonder-type-specific flavor to all other countries with capitals, clears temporary project variables and IO progress back to zero, and then stops support decay. Finalized wonder buildings have `max_levels = 6`, cannot be destroyed, hide normal player construction behind `country_potential = { always = no }` plus the "Wonders can only be built through the Engineering Department" allow tooltip, and carry only designed shared/base local effects; the old generic style-2 branch-specific local bonuses now live on the auxiliary annex buildings instead of the finished wonders. National/base/ritual effects are applied by permanent country modifiers instead of building country effects because wonders may not be in the capital. Support and module buildings can still carry their own local effects. When wonder construction terminates in any form, completed survey data, final buildings, leftover module buildings, and completed annex buildings persist. Future Great Engineer agendas prioritize half-built wonders, extra modules, and finalized wonders below their stored site cap; upgrades reuse the previous ceremony style and can claim completion rewards again. The custom IO panel hides the Members tab; its overview tab contains active conception and debate controls; its resolutions tab contains Survey plus Organization/Logistics; and its custom tab contains Construction plus Ceremony controls.

The **Trade Victory** path (贸易胜利) is fully implemented with a Trade League layer: 5 triggers, 5 reward modifiers, 5 events, and localization in English and Simplified Chinese. Score = monthly_trade_income (snapshot taken monthly by `tv_update_trade_score_effect`); thresholds at 100 / 200 / 400 / 1000 / 2000. M3 and M5 additionally require the country to be the strongest trader in at least 5 / 10 markets. **Trade League IO** (`tv_trade_league`): non-unique IO created by the Trade Victory M1 reward; the founder is added as a member, receives `tv_trade_league_member`, and remains the locked leader country (`leader_change_trigger_type = none`, `leader_change_method = none`). The appointed **Grand Merchant** is stored on the leader country as `tv_grand_merchant_char` and managed by generated Appoint/Remove/Change leader actions. The leader can invite countries through `tv_invite_to_trade_league` (base AI acceptance -200, opinion scaling 0.5, republic +20, merchant republic +30, <=5 locations +20, leader diplomatic reputation x2, leader monthly trade income / 10) and expel non-leader members through `tv_expel_trade_league_member`. The `tv_trade_league_member_status` special status gives non-leader members `global_merchant_capacity_modifier = -0.10`, while the leader gains `global_merchant_capacity_modifier = 0.02` per non-leader member through `leader_modifier`. `tv_trade_league_laws.txt` adds five generated three-level vote-based law groups, but the generated IO definition starts with no enacted laws; the policies are enacted later through votes.

Trade League monopoly tracking and actions are generated from `data/trade_league_goods.yaml`, which also defines the monopoly-tab display groups: Basic Goods, Food, Manufactured Goods, and Luxury Goods. Per good, the monopoly flag, total monopoly percentage, origin/node/consumer aggregate control percentages, top-3 market slot variables, and persistent virtual-demand, virtual-supply, and embargo state are stored on the Trade League `leader_country`, not in the IO type `variables = {}` block; initializing thousands of IO variables caused severe in-game stutter. The generated Trade League IO type also avoids an IO `monthly_effect`; `tv_trade_league_monthly_pulse` runs from `monthly_country_pulse`, lets each country check only the Trade League IOs it belongs to, and enters only IOs where `leader_country` is that country before calling the IO-scoped monopoly refresh. That maintenance only proceeds while the Grand Merchant exists, is alive, and passes the Grand Merchant availability trigger, then saves the IO as `scope:tv_trade_monopoly_io` and enters `leader_country` for all state writes. Trade Chain and Commercial Intelligence are placeholders, but `tv_trade_league_triggers.txt` already defines the Grand Merchant busy-state flags (`tv_trade_chain_active`, `tv_trade_commercial_intelligence_active`, and active `resolution:policy_vote` for law changes) and mutual-exclusion triggers so those future systems, Trade League law votes, and the monopoly report disable one another consistently. Monopoly tracking is split into annual global marker refresh and monthly IO refresh. Annual refresh ranks and stores market-center locations plus static slot metrics: origin markets by `produced_in_market:<good>`, node markets by summing market `every_export` routes with matching `goods` and `trade_volume`, and consumer markets by `goods_demand_in_market(goods:<good>)`; it also stores total export/import route denominators for the slots. Monthly IO refresh revisits only those marked markets, sums member-owned route numerators with market `every_export` for origin/node control and market `every_import` for consumer control, stores slot and aggregate percentages on the leader country, and marks `tv_trade_monopoly_<good>` when `tv_trade_monopoly_level_pct_<good> >= 100`. Each aggregate is the average of three slot controls, missing or zero-denominator slots count as 0, and the total monopoly percentage is the sum of the origin, node, and consumer aggregate controls, capped naturally at 300%. The generated projection effects copy the active category/page into 15 fixed display rows (`tv_trade_display_row_<n>_*`) and copy the selected good into generic detail variables such as `tv_trade_selected_good_scope`, `tv_trade_selected_monopoly_level_pct`, and `tv_trade_selected_origin_market_<rank>`. The UI reads only these projected variables through `InternationalOrganizationsView.GetInternationalOrganization.GetLeaderCountry.MakeScope.GetVariable(...)` and no longer displays world production, IO trade, `traded_in_market`, or `goods_supply_in_market` for the monopoly report.

The monopoly action layer generates category-select, previous/next page, and 15 fixed row-select actions plus one fixed selected-good action set: set virtual demand or virtual production through market + value `select_trigger`s, increase/decrease/cancel either virtual action, set an embargo through market + country `select_trigger`s, and cancel the embargo. Those fixed actions dispatch by `tv_trade_selected_good` into generated per-good script branches, so the GUI does not instantiate per-good buttons. Each point of virtual demand or production locks 5% monopoly level, while each embargo locks 30%. Virtual demand uses generated `goods_demand` entries and is applied as `add_temporary_demand` with `months = -1`; set/increase/decrease/cancel actions remove and reapply it as needed, monthly monopoly maintenance removes it when the monopoly is lost, and the Trade League monthly fallback removes all virtual demand while the Grand Merchant cannot maintain monopolies. Virtual production now follows the same persistent-action pattern experimentally: the leader country tracks `tv_trade_virtual_supply_applied_<good>`, applies `add_goods_supply` once when active, removes it by applying a negative `add_goods_supply` amount before changing/canceling/suspending, and reapplies it when maintenance conditions recover. Embargo applies a 2-month `merchant_power_severe_penalty` through `add_merchant_power`. Action variables persist on the leader country when the monopoly is lost, but effects are applied only while `tv_trade_monopoly_<good> >= 1`. Every generated action is also registered in `tv_trade_league_list.txt` and `messagetypes.txt`. The Trade League IO panel is generated: the header manages the Grand Merchant appointment/replacement/dismissal, the overview shows Grand Merchant status, member invite/expel controls, and a placeholder trade-chain card; the renamed Resolutions tab shows a commercial-intelligence placeholder; the custom tab shows category and page controls above 15 projected row slots, with each row retaining icon/name, origin/node/consumer control, reserved monopoly checkmark, and total monopoly percentage. Selecting a row refreshes a single detail card with a piechart, monopoly/used progress bars scaled to 300%, top-3 origin/node/consumer market tables, active action rows, and one leader-only fixed action card for monopolized goods. Detail rows show local demand, local production, total export/import, and IO export/import using the projected slot variables copied from the selected good; origin/node rows project `tv_trade_<prefix>_total_export_<rank>_<good>` and `tv_trade_<prefix>_io_export_<rank>_<good>`, while consumer rows project `tv_trade_consumer_total_import_<rank>_<good>` and `tv_trade_consumer_io_import_<rank>_<good>`.

The **Diplomatic Victory** path (外交胜利) is fully implemented: 5 triggers, 5 reward modifiers, 5 milestone events, and localization in English and Simplified Chinese. M1 requires 2 diplomatic supporters and 1 DVP, but its reward no longer creates the Diplomatic Alliance IO. The second accepted Diplomatic Support pledge triggers the visible flavor event `tv_diplo_alliance.2`; that event's option calls `tv_create_diplomatic_alliance_effect`, creates the founder's non-unique Diplomatic Alliance IO, enrolls all existing supporters, and ensures DVP is at least 1.

Arts Exhibition filters candidates by `is_artist = yes`; Academy, Engineering Department, and Trade League exclude artists; Diplomatic Alliance accepts any non-ruler/heir/consort alive adult character; Grand General additionally excludes female characters and artists. Six CMF alerts (`tv_missing_arts_exhibition_chief_alert`, `tv_missing_diplomatic_alliance_chief_alert`, `tv_missing_academy_chief_alert`, `tv_missing_engineering_department_chief_alert`, `tv_missing_trade_league_chief_alert`, `tv_missing_govhouse_chief_alert`) are checked monthly for human players and shown whenever the matching TV IO lacks a living appointed chief; their tooltips direct the player to open the matching organization panel and appoint the chief. **Character title prefixes (Arts Exhibition + Academy + Governor's House + Engineering Department + Trade League):** Appointing/removing leaders also adds/removes zero-stat character modifiers (`tv_chief_artist_role_modifier` / `tv_chief_scientist_role_modifier` / `tv_grand_general_role_modifier` / `tv_great_engineer_role_modifier` / `tv_grand_merchant_role_modifier`). Modifier definitions are in `src/in_game/common/static_modifiers/tv_io_role_modifiers.txt`. The Diplomatic Alliance leader has no title modifier.
10. **Governor's House IO** (`tv_govhouse`): non-unique IO created by the Conquest Victory M1 reward (200 locations). **Governor system:** characters appointed by the player to govern EU5 regions. Governor bonuses scale dynamically with character attributes, but the exact local modifier is selected by the three **Local Governor Functions** IO laws. Administrative Function options: `tv_governor_max_control` (`local_max_control`, adm × 0.001, default), `tv_governor_monthly_development` (`local_monthly_development`, adm × 0.00001), or `tv_governor_production_efficiency` (`local_production_efficiency`, adm × 0.001). Diplomatic Function options: `tv_governor_build_cost` (`local_build_buildings_cost`, −dip × 0.002, default), `tv_governor_pop_promotion_speed` (`local_pop_promotion_speed_modifier`, dip × 0.005), or `tv_governor_population_growth` (`local_population_growth`, dip × 0.00001). Military Function options: `tv_governor_unrest` (`local_unrest`, −mil × 0.002, default), `tv_governor_levy_size` (`local_army_levy_size_modifier`, mil × 0.002), or `tv_governor_fort_defense` (`local_defensive`, mil × 0.005). Every regional governor also applies `tv_governor_integration_speed` (`local_integration_speed`, ADM × 0.001) to owned locations in the governed region; the Governor's House overview card displays this as a separate left-side slot before the three law-selected function slots. Data model: `tv_govhouse_region_list` is a persistent country variable list of region scopes rebuilt from the country's currently owned locations at IO creation and every monthly governor refresh; `tv_govhouse_region_display_list` is a parallel country variable list of representative owned location scopes used by the GUI to display one row per owned region; `tv_governor_list` remains a persistent country variable list of appointed governor character scopes. Each governor character has `tv_is_governor`, `tv_governed_region` (region scope), `tv_governed_region_capital` (representative owned location retained for compatibility), and pre-computed function display variables (`tv_gov_admin_bonus_type/value`, `tv_gov_dip_bonus_type/value`, `tv_gov_mil_bonus_type/value`). The representative display location mirrors the active governor and copied function values via `tv_govhouse_display_governor_char` plus `tv_govhouse_display_{admin/dip/mil}_bonus_{type/value}`. Monthly_effect in the IO calls `tv_apply_governor_bonuses_effect`, which refreshes modifiers for alive governors, prunes dead/orphaned/lost-region assignments, and then rebuilds the region/display lists; legacy `tv_governed_area` assignments are migrated/cleaned during refresh. Three region-row generic actions: `tv_appoint_governor` (UI passes `scope:target_1` region, select_trigger chooses `scope:target` character), `tv_remove_governor` (UI passes region and the effect finds the assigned governor), and `tv_change_governor` (region parameter + character selection, replacing any existing assignment). The overview-level `tv_auto_assign_governors` action refreshes the region list, then repeatedly chooses one empty owned region and the highest `total_abilities` eligible character until every owned region is filled or no eligible character remains; all governor management actions require an alive Grand General in office. If the Grand General is dismissed or dies, existing governors remain appointed and continue refreshing, but no new governor can be appointed/changed/removed until a successor is appointed. **Grand General:** character leader stored as the independent country variable `tv_conquest_general_char`; managed via generated Appoint/Remove/Change IO leader actions and displayed in the IO header. Newly appointed Grand Generals initialize `tv_gg_loyalty` on the character if absent (nobles 0%, burghers/clergy 10%, crown 30%, other estates 20%); the value is mirrored to `tv_gg_loyalty_value`, does not drift automatically, and lowers the Governor's House power-balance equilibrium by the same amount. A monthly 5% loyalty pulse can fire one of three events: -5 loyalty, +5 loyalty, or a reward demand costing `scale = 1` gold for +5 loyalty with refusal causing -5 loyalty. `tv_govhouse_authority` IO variable (0-100, monthly decay × 0.0005, grows by Grand General's mil × 0.05). The Governor's House overview tab renders the display-location list: each row shows a region icon/name, the separate integration slot, right-aligned ADM/DIP/MIL function entries with formula tooltips, and the current governor as a standard `io_character_card`; Assign/Cancel/Change Governor buttons still pass the row's region scope. The dedicated Grand General tab shows the current variable-backed character, active training progress below the character card, authority value, Appoint/Change/Remove actions, and the existing Train Grand General action.

Governor automation note: `tv_enable_governor_automation` / `tv_disable_governor_automation` toggle the country variable `tv_govhouse_governor_automation_enabled` from the overview `button_checkbox_auto` checkbutton beside `tv_auto_assign_governors`. When enabled, governor death, manual removal, location ownership changes, governor country moves, a governor becoming ruler, and marriage/royal-marriage cases where a governor becomes a consort each run one guarded `tv_govhouse_auto_assign_if_enabled_effect` pass; that helper delegates to `tv_governor_auto_assign_effect` only while a living Grand General is available, so the automation does not rely on monthly polling.

Governor assignment country note: new Local Governor assignments store `tv_governor_country` on the character so event-driven vacancy cleanup can find the original appointing country even if the character's current owner changes.

Governor autonomous task note: `tv_governor_select_yearly_task_effect` sets one active task per regional governor. The six tasks are restore prosperity, integrate region, convert culture, convert religion, strengthen recruitment, and strengthen order; integration is one of these autonomous tasks, not a separate always-on governor modifier. The task values scale from ADM/DIP/MIL, are displayed through `tv_govhouse_display_task_type/value`, and are refreshed into region-wide location modifiers by `tv_governor_apply_modifiers_effect`.

Regional Governor random events: `tv_govhouse_governor_events_monthly_pulse` runs through the generated `monthly_country_pulse` registry and only triggers if at least one living governor has an owned governed region. It uses a single monthly country-level roll with 180 no-event weight plus 20 event weights, giving a 10% total monthly chance. Events `tv_govhouse.100`-`.119` each pick one random eligible governor in `immediate`, save the governor, governed region, and a representative owned location as event scopes, then apply one-year region-wide location modifiers and/or immediate or one-year country effects. The 20-event set covers two-choice good events, one-option good events, two-choice tradeoff events, two-choice bad events, and one-option bad events.

Governor's House laws: the IO uses the vanilla IO Laws tab. Six generated core law groups (`tv_govhouse_expedition_goal_law`, `tv_govhouse_governor_appointment_law`, `tv_govhouse_local_autonomy_law`, `tv_govhouse_religious_mission_law`, `tv_govhouse_frontier_military_law`, `tv_govhouse_imperial_integration_law`) each have four effect-bearing country policies. `tv_govhouse_grand_general_power_law` sits in the new `tv_govhouse_grand_general` category: Marching Orders gives newly appointed Grand Generals +10% initial loyalty, Garrison Orders makes governor task/function effects 20% stronger, and Supreme Command gives newly appointed Grand Generals -10% initial loyalty while doubling General Tactics and War Plan progress gain. Four generated Local Governor Function laws sit in `tv_govhouse_governor_functions`: the ADM/DIP/MIL function laws decide which scaled local modifiers governors apply, while `tv_govhouse_governor_duty_tendency_law` adds +0.30 task-selection weight to one of the six autonomous governor tasks. All eleven laws keep `requires_vote = yes`, `has_parliament = no` hides the separate IO Parliament tab, and the IO starts with no initial policies. There is no monthly default-law backfill.

Governor's House War Tent: requires an alive Grand General. The custom War Tent tab has three units: General Tactics, War Plan, and Main Actions. On war entry, `tv_govhouse_war_entry_effect` proposes one random tactic from the eleven-tactic pool and fires `tv_govhouse.40`; the player approves or rejects it. While continuously at war, the monthly pulse has a 5% chance to propose a replacement tactic, excluding the tactic already active. Approved tactics are applied as country-scoped static modifiers and are cleared when the country leaves all wars or loses its Grand General; this approximates general tactics because the army-wide combat modifiers did not apply reliably from the stored character path. Supreme Command doubles the applied tactic modifier size, including the Capital Raid location modifier. War plans are mutually exclusive: offensive plans select a target country and, while at war with that target, scale Siege Ability by progress x 0.2% and Discipline by progress x 0.1%; defensive plans need no target and, while in any defensive war, scale Fort Defense by progress x 0.2% and Military Tactics by progress x 0.005. On war entry, applicable offensive/defensive plan bonuses are activated through `tv_govhouse.41` / `tv_govhouse.42` events instead of being granted directly by the war-entry effect. Plan progress increases monthly by Grand General adm x 0.02 up to 100, doubled under Supreme Command; aborting/changing target resets it, and peace with an offensive plan target aborts that plan.

8. **Global Victory Leaderboard** — Monthly ranking of the top 5 countries by their best single-path progress (0–100 bar value). Per-country: `tv_best_path` (0–5), `tv_best_progress_pct`, `tv_best_milestone`, `tv_best_score` computed via `tv_update_best_path_effect` in `monthly_country_pulse`. Situation stores rank-1..5 country references (`tv_rank_1_country`–`tv_rank_5_country`) via `ordered_in_global_list` cascading-exclusion pattern. Displayed in Section 3 with flag, country name, leading-path circle icon, milestone count, and score text.

Governor's House eligibility/death note: new governors and Grand Generals exclude female characters and artists at the selection/action-effect entry points. There is no monthly legacy-eligibility cleanup, to keep the IO monthly path focused on active governor bonus refresh. `tv_govhouse_governor_death` is registered under `on_character_death`, fires `tv_govhouse.1`, removes the dead governor from `tv_governor_list`, clears their region modifiers, and rebuilds the display lists immediately.

Grand General training note: `tv_train_grand_general` applies the one-year `tv_grand_general_training_modifier` country modifier defined in `src/in_game/common/static_modifiers/tv_govhouse_modifiers.txt` (+10% `court_spending_cost_modifier`), tracks `tv_gg_training_months` on the Grand General via monthly pulse, mirrors it to the country-side GUI display variable `tv_gg_training_progress`, resolves at 12 months through `tv_training_resolve_effect`, and routes to skill gain, trait-path upgrade, injury, or death events before clearing state.

Grand General death notification note: natural Grand General deaths trigger `tv_govhouse.2` from `tv_govhouse_char_death`; training-caused deaths set `tv_gg_training_death_notified` before `kill_character = this` so the already-shown training death event is not duplicated.

Governor's House balance-of-power backlash note: `tv_govhouse_power_balance` is an IO variable in the 0..1 range and is checked monthly by `tv_govhouse_negative_monthly_pulse` (registered through `data/pulse_registry.yaml`). `tv_govhouse_power_equilibrium` recalculates monthly from a -20% base, the Grand General's share of combined ruler/general abilities, `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation`, and a subtraction equal to the mirrored Grand General loyalty value; `tv_govhouse_power_balance` then moves toward that equilibrium by up to 0.6% per month. Below 25% no backlash events fire. At 25%/50%/75% the pulse uses a single `random_list` roll with a 300 total weight so at most one backlash event can appear per month, and each eligible event has weight 1/300. Events `tv_govhouse.10`-`.13` are tier 1, `.20`-`.25` are tier 2, and `.30`-`.34` are tier 3. Every backlash event has exactly two options: accept the negative effect, or pay an alternative resource and call `tv_govhouse_reduce_power_balance_effect` to lower the balance by 5 percentage points. Tier 2 adds rare art/artist losses; tier 3 upgrades the local satisfaction loss to an area-wide effect and adds a cabinet crisis. The Governor's House GUI shows right-aligned fixed-width 25/50/75/100% markers and four warning rows below the Balance of Power bar for the <25, 25-49, 50-74, and 75-100 segments.

Grand General replacement cost note: generated Remove/Change Grand General actions call `tv_govhouse_pay_grand_general_replacement_cost_effect` before resetting `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation`; the cost is Government Power equal to `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation * 20`, so a fully consolidated Grand General costs 20 government power to remove or replace.

## IO Architecture Design Decisions

The character-led TV IOs (`tv_arts_exhibition`, `tv_diplomatic_alliance`, `tv_academy_of_sciences`, `tv_govhouse`, `tv_engineering_department`, `tv_trade_league`) share invariants that must never be broken.

### 1. leader_country = founding country, locked at creation

`on_creation = { set_leader_country = scope:actor }` runs once when the IO is created. `leader_country` is not reassigned by normal gameplay afterward; the only exception is the manual recovery action `tv_reinitialize_mod`, exposed at the top of the Victory Situation panel, which recreates missing victory-path IOs where possible and reassigns joined TV IO instances back to the current country. It controls IO permissions, `is_leader_of_international_organization` trigger, and `leader_country.modifier:` chained access in `monthly_change` scripted values.

### 2. No elections — leader_change_trigger_type = none is mandatory

All TV character-led IO type definitions keep `leader_change_trigger_type = none` and `leader_change_method = none`. These prevent the engine from reassigning `leader_country` automatically. `international_organization_chooses_new_leader` is **globally banned** from all TV IO-related code — this effect triggers the vanilla election process. Any call to it violates the no-elections design and risks silently reassigning `leader_country` to a different country.

### 3. Great person characters are independent country variables, not the vanilla ruler

The character displayed in the IO header is stored as a country variable on the `leader_country`:
- Arts Exhibition: `tv_arts_exhibition_leader_char`
- Diplomatic Alliance: `tv_diplomatic_alliance_leader_char`
- Academy of Sciences: `tv_academy_leader_char`
- Governor's House: `tv_conquest_general_char`
- Engineering Department: `tv_great_engineer_char`
- Trade League: `tv_grand_merchant_char`

These are set/cleared exclusively by the generated generic actions in `tv_io_leader_actions.txt`. The vanilla `leader_country.GetGovernment.GetRuler` and `appointed_leader.*` are different concepts and must not be used in TV IO calculations.

### 4. IO variable monthly_change uses chained character attribute access

All IO variable `monthly_change` scripted values that depend on the appointed great person must use `leader_country.var:tv_xxx_leader_char.attribute` chained access — never `appointed_leader.attribute`. Examples:
- CORRECT: `value = leader_country.var:tv_academy_leader_char.adm`
- FORBIDDEN: `value = appointed_leader.adm`

### 5. Header display uses blockoverride

All character-led IO panels use `blockoverride` on their header template to display the appointed character (read from the country variable via `GetPlayer.MakeScope.GetVariable(...).GetCharacter`). Never use the vanilla `GetLeaderCountry.GetGovernment.GetRulerOrRegent` accessor chain.

### 6. All TV IOs have unique = no

Each country pursuing the relevant victory path creates its own independent IO instance. The `international_organization:type_name` scope link is **invalid** for non-unique types. Always use `every_international_organizations_member_of = { limit = { international_organization_type = international_organization_type:<io_type> } ... }`.

## AI Workflow Knowledge

AI workflow state is maintained as project source, not by manual human follow-up. `scripts/ai_context.py` builds the task-scoped context from changed or explicit files, generated-file metadata, `docs/knowledge/anti_patterns.yaml`, and short domain risk cards in `docs/knowledge/risk_cards/`. Current registered risk cards: `events.md`, `generic_actions.md`, `gui.md`, `international_organizations.md`, and `on_action.md`; event files route to the events card because option tooltips can pre-evaluate visible effect chains, while IO definitions, IO laws, and country interactions that find or mutate TV IOs route to the international-organization card because IO scopes are high risk. Anti-pattern entries use `detectability` to distinguish safe regex lint (`lint`), parser/semantic rules (`needs_parser`), and AI-facing warnings (`advisory`). `scripts/validate.py` enforces safe lint rules, emits generic-action pre-evaluation and interaction-target chooser warnings, rejects visible generic-action calls to registered hidden-only cleanup helpers, emits IO policy AI-scope warnings/errors, fails duplicate main-menu localization keys within the same language pack, checks that risk cards remain registered in `ai_context.py`, checks that each TV IO type has a matching `main_menu/gfx/interface/icons/international_organizations/<io_type>.dds` GetIcon asset, and checks that TV does not stack a second direct `effect` onto hardcoded vanilla on_action hooks such as `on_annex` or `on_winning_war`. `scripts/test_lint_rules.py` runs fixture tests from `tests/fixtures/anti_patterns/<rule_id>/` for selected lint regexes.

For Engineering Department wonders, remember the noncapital building rule: EU5 building `modifier` / `raw_modifier` are local, while `capital_country_modifier` only applies country effects if the building is in the capital. Since wonders can be outside the capital, final wonder buildings must contain only designed local effects, and all national/global effects must be applied through permanent country modifiers during finalization.

Wonder Construction random events are data-driven from `data/wonder_construction_events.yaml`. The generated outputs are `src/in_game/events/tv_wonder_construction_events.txt`, `src/in_game/common/scripted_effects/tv_wonder_construction_event_effects.txt`, `src/in_game/common/scripted_triggers/tv_wonder_construction_event_triggers.txt`, and the matching English/Simplified Chinese localization files. Re-run all five `gen_tv_wonder_construction_events*` generators after changing token values, event categories, weights, eligibility rules, or flavor text.

TV-authored `trigger`/`limit` condition blocks that use `has_variable` may wrap guards in `custom_tooltip`, but generated vanilla-copy files must not rewrite copied vanilla guards.

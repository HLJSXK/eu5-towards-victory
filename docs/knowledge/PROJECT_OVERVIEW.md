# Towards Victory — Project Overview

## Mod Identity

- **Mod Name:** Towards Victory (胜利条件)
- **Mod ID:** `eu5mp.towards_victory`
- **Version:** Build date (`YYMMDD`)
- **Target:** EU5 `1.*.*`
- **Status:** In Development — All 6 Victory paths fully implemented: Conquest, Prosperity, Trade, Diplomatic, Cultural, and Scientific
- **Language:** English + Simplified Chinese

## Development Policy

Towards Victory is an unreleased project with date-based `YYMMDD` versions. No saved-game compatibility, published
mod-version compatibility, or public API compatibility is required. Active code and data
should represent one current architecture. When a refactor improves clarity, performance,
generator ownership, or data flow, update all producers and consumers to the new shape and
delete stale branches rather than keeping migrations, aliases, duplicate paths, wrapper
entry points, or defensive fallbacks for old internal states. Existing helpers should be
reused only when they fit the current scope/lifecycle/data model; otherwise split, replace,
or remove them.

Existing mentions of compatibility launchers, legacy schema blocks, migrated old variables,
startup/migration repair, or fields retained for compatibility describe cleanup debt, not
contracts. When touching those areas, convert callers/data to the current shape and remove
the old path instead of preserving the compatibility layer.

Governor's House subject-chain lifecycle: only independent countries may own a Governor's House. When an owner becomes a subject, its owned house is dissolved and owner-only Grand General, Governor, War Tent, and automation state is cleared; player countries see a confirmation popup while AI countries resolve silently. A top overlord's owned Governor's House automatically includes every subject below it as IO members without setting `tv_govhouse_member` on those subject members; the locked `leader_country` receives `tv_govhouse_supreme_overlord_status` (Supreme Overlord / 最高宗主), while every attached subject-chain member receives `tv_govhouse_subject_status` (Subject / 附属). When a former subject becomes independent, Conquest score is recalculated; countries that newly meet Conquest Milestone 1 use the normal milestone reward path, while countries that already had that milestone rebuild only the Governor's House.

Diplomatic Alliance subject-chain lifecycle mirrors the same owner rule while preserving the alliance's ordinary member semantics: only independent countries can establish or restore their own Diplomatic Alliance, and an owned alliance automatically attaches every subject and lower subject as IO members. When an alliance owner becomes or transfers as a subject, its owned alliance is dissolved, owner-only Advocator/member-count/cache state is cleared, and the attached subject chain is removed from external Diplomatic Alliances before the new top overlord's alliance reattaches it. A former owner marked during dissolution rebuilds its Diplomatic Alliance after regaining independence. The leader-country `tv_diplomatic_alliance_member_count` cache is refreshed from the IO's `total_members` at creation, joining, leaving, subject-chain sync, and explicit cleanup points.

Governor's House regional administration lifecycle: `tv_govhouse_region_to_admin_location_map` is a stable country cache from region to the system-designated Local Administration location, not a scan result from existing buildings. Startup/load, ownership changes, capital moves, and Local Administration confirmation maintain the cache; monthly logic does not rebuild it. Non-capital regions select an admin point only when missing or when the old point leaves the country, preferring highest-population city/megalopolis, then town, then rural settlement. Selected locations carry `tv_govhouse_local_admin_owner` and `tv_govhouse_admin_region` markers so ownership-change hooks can delete transferred Local Administration buildings, clear the losing country's map/governor assignment, and let the winner refresh its own cache without inheriting the building. Governor display and modifier maintenance are split from admin-point selection: monthly random governor events read `tv_govhouse_has_valid_governor`, while yearly/law/lifecycle hooks refresh governor modifiers.

Towards Victory adds 6 generalized victory paths to EU5: Conquest, Prosperity, Trade, Diplomatic, Cultural, and Scientific. Each path has 5 milestone nodes, and each milestone offers 3 independent permanent reward modifiers selected through the reward buttons. Reaching a milestone unlocks its reward slot and fires a popup event for notification/unlock-only side effects; permanent buffs are applied only through the generated three-choice reward buttons. Each path's progress is initially gated by `tv_<path>_victory_enabled` and normally unlocks independently only after that path's IO establishment flow is complete; Trade and Diplomatic Victory can also unlock immediately when a country joins another country's Trade League or Diplomatic Alliance. The main victory situation is a generated single-path page with a six-button top switcher backed by the player country variable `tv_victory_selected_path`; disabled paths show a three-step establishment card, while enabled paths show the per-path overview card with a concept-linked title, playstyle bullet, progress-formula bullet, progress and reward cards, and a per-path global top-five leaderboard.

The new Victory Path Tree task layer is data-driven by `data/victory_path_tasks.yaml`. Each enabled path gives human players three task slots between the illustrated node tree and the legacy path overview; enabling a path initializes its tree points to 0 and assigns all three slots, with no initial 1000-point grant and no monthly free-point trickle. The current pool contains 77 fixed and objective tasks across the six paths (10 Conquest, 10 Prosperity, 11 Trade, 9 Diplomatic, 13 Cultural, and 24 Scientific). Fixed families snapshot the first threshold strictly above both the last claimed tier and the country's current value, while claimed objective tasks are permanently excluded. Assignment rejects tasks already complete at refresh time, excludes families used by the other two slots, prefers eligible national-special tasks, and otherwise chooses ordinary candidates with equal weight; national-special `x2xx` IDs and current-or-former-tag routing exist, but their candidate pools are intentionally empty for now. Empty slots use task ID 0 and retry during the human monthly pulse.

Task completion is latched in slot state and is claimed through one of 18 fixed Generic Actions. A claim grants exactly 1 point to the matching path's node tree, records the fixed tier or one-time objective completion, and refreshes only that slot; enabling a route refreshes all three slots. Every occupied task card also binds a separate right-click Generic Action: when the human player has at least 10 Prestige, it spends 10 Prestige and immediately refreshes only that card's slot, without awarding a tree point. Cheap state tasks and empty slots are checked only for active human slots on the monthly pulse, technology counts reuse the yearly scan, and ownership/building/institution/war/artwork/organization/MOD actions use targeted callbacks. Monthly task IDs are ordered first in each slot update router. Empty slots on the same path share one candidate-metric refresh pass, skip metrics for fixed families whose final tier is already claimed, reuse the prepared metrics when a task is assigned, and stop trying later empty slots after the first slot finds no eligible candidate; this preserves monthly retry behavior without repeating equivalent location, character, market, organization, or candidate-pool scans up to three times. Expensive cultural and geographic unification checks run only during assignment and ownership-change callbacks. The nine price-event objectives are appended through generated same-ID vanilla event copies; the three global-once objectives (Triangular Trade, European Merchants in China, and Spice Trade) complete for the first eligible holder, fail and refresh competing human holders, and never return to the pool after the global event flag is set. Italian War objectives complete for members of the actual winning alliance before vanilla destroys the alliance state, while losing or drawn holders fail and refresh; Black Death holders complete only if they actually experienced the situation, and other holders fail when it ends. Task failure is silent and never awards tree points.

IO establishment is data-driven by `data/io_establishment.yaml`. Every path uses the same three-step lifecycle: a monthly pulse completes the basic requirement flag, fires a player guidance event, and refreshes the headquarters-built cache; the guidance event or the capital-only `select_trigger` action can start that path's 300-ducat headquarters with themed maintenance goods; headquarters completion is marked from the building `on_construction_ended` hook and fires a second guidance event to appoint the chief; and the appoint-chief action initializes the country leader variable, applies the chief modifier, creates the matching non-unique IO, sets `tv_<path>_victory_enabled = 1`, fires the generated establishment event, and immediately refreshes that path's victory progress. AI countries use the same generated build-HQ and appoint-chief actions through `generic_action_ai_lists`, action-native `ai_tick`, and `ai_will_do`; the monthly pulse no longer simulates those action effects. Diplomatic AI countries complete step 1 at 10 diplomatic reputation like players, but only execute the step-2 headquarters build action at 20 diplomatic reputation or higher while fewer than 10 Diplomatic Alliances already exist worldwide; Trade AI countries complete step 1 at 50 monthly trade income like players, but only execute the step-2 headquarters build action at 100 monthly trade income or higher and while fewer than 10 Trade League leader countries exist globally. The step-3 appoint-chief actions have no extra diplomatic-reputation or trade-income gate once the headquarters exists, though Trade AI still obeys the global Trade League cap.

The **Trade Victory** path (贸易胜利) is fully implemented with a Trade League layer: 5 triggers, 15 selectable reward modifiers, 5 events, and localization in English and Simplified Chinese. Trade League monopoly tracking and actions are generated from the pure goods list in `data/trade_league_goods.yaml`, with Trade Chain configuration in `data/trade_chain.yaml`. Per good, the final monopoly percentage, monopoly type, stage, origin/transit/consumer control percentages, representative market detail variables, persistent virtual-demand, virtual-supply, embargo state, and Trade Chain bargaining demand state are stored on the Trade League `leader_country`, not in the IO type `variables = {}` block; initializing thousands of IO variables caused severe in-game stutter. The generated Trade League IO type also avoids an IO `monthly_effect`; Trade League maintenance is routed through registered monthly and yearly country pulses in `data/pulse_registry.yaml` plus the manual `tv_trade_league_on_action.txt` bridge. Grand Merchant work is mutually exclusive through `tv_trade_league_triggers.txt`: commercial intelligence (`tv_trade_commercial_intelligence_active`), active `resolution:policy_vote`, and monopoly maintenance availability block one another as appropriate. Trade Chain is no longer a Grand Merchant busy state. When none of those implemented busy states is active, `tv_trade_league_assist_caravans_modifier` applies as a leader-country Country Auto modifier for +10% merchant maintenance efficiency and is shown as the header action-card fallback; the planned promote-monopoly action has no display state yet and is not surfaced.

Trade Chain is a 20-slot persistent leader-country market chain. Canonical state uses `tv_trade_chain_market_by_slot` as a variable map keyed by slot number with market-scope values, plus the ordered `tv_trade_chain_markets` variable list for GUI iteration; `tv_trade_chain_count` includes the capital node. Slot 1 is always the current capital market. `tv_trade_add_chain_node` selects one market at a time; the selector and final effect require trade range, no duplicate existing chain node, and for the next node's market center to be strictly closer to the previous node's market center than to any other existing chain node's center. Creation, monthly maintenance, and capital-change handling ensure or rebuild slot 1 from the current capital market. The `on_capital_moved` bridge resets the chain only when the old and new capital markets differ, clears old permanent temporary bargaining demands, and notifies human players through `tv_trade_league_chain.1`. Monthly maintenance computes `flow = n / Σ(1 / market_capacity)`, sets flow to 0 if any chain market has non-positive leader capacity, measures endpoint distance from the capital market center to the terminal market center, and stores `strength = floor(length × flow / 1000)`. Strength at 100 grants `tv_trade_chain_medium_modifier` (+10% merchant maintenance efficiency); strength at 1000 also grants `tv_trade_chain_strong_modifier` (+20% trade advantage). Each point of strength contributes 0.01% display-only `tv_trade_bargaining_efficiency`; the GUI dynamically resolves `ShowModifierEffect(Concatenate('tv_trade_chain_strength_display_', strength_display))` against generated `tv_trade_chain_strength_display_0..20000` static modifiers instead of emitting one display widget per strength. Each month the chain clears old permanent temporary `tv_trade_chain_bargaining_demand_<good>` demands, then clears and rebuilds `tv_trade_chain_demand_slot_1..20` variable maps keyed by `goods:<id>` scope. From back to front it records exports from node n to node n-1 and adds `trade_volume × strength × 0.0001` demand to every preceding node 1..n-1 for that good.

Monopoly tracking uses an annual global good refresh, yearly per-league full recalculation, and monthly slot-only recalculation. The annual global refresh writes keyed `global_variable_map` entries for each good index: global production, global consumption, representative origin/transit/consumer markets, representative static display metrics, and effective-market denominators. It also maintains three global lists per good: origin-effective markets whose production is at least 20% of the highest production market, transit-effective markets whose export is at least 20% of the highest export market, and consumer-effective markets whose demand is at least 20% of the highest demand market; zero-value markets are excluded. Origin and transit denominators are total export across their effective lists, and the consumer denominator is total import across its effective list. Representative markets are only detail-page decision support. The yearly Trade League pulse refreshes the global cache once per year through a global version/cooldown, then each eligible Trade League leader that has not seen that version recomputes every tracked good and refills monopoly slots. The monthly pulse recomputes only goods already stored in unlocked or previously-filled monopoly slots, using `every_in_global_list` over the effective market lists rather than scanning every world market. Origin and transit control divide member export from effective markets by the cached effective total export; consumer control divides member import from effective markets by the cached effective total import. The final `tv_trade_monopoly_level_pct_<good>` is the maximum of those three percentages, capped at 100, with `tv_trade_monopoly_type_<good>` set to 1 origin, 2 transit, or 3 consumer. `tv_trade_monopoly_stage_<good>` is 0 none, 1 potential at 25%, 2 low at 50%, 3 high at 75%, and 4 complete at 100%. Annual full recalculation auto-fills monopoly slots from goods at or above 25% by final percentage descending, with YAML order as the stable tie-breaker; monthly refresh updates the existing slot values but does not reselect goods. The Trade League has 1 slot by default and 2 slots once membership reaches 10; goods outside unlocked slots are not visible and provide no monopoly action benefit. `tv_trade_monopoly_<good>` is set only when the good is in an unlocked slot and has at least 50% final monopoly progress.

The monopoly/action layer generates two slot-select actions plus one fixed selected-good action set: set virtual demand or virtual production through market + value `select_trigger`s, increase/decrease/cancel either virtual action, set an embargo through market + country `select_trigger`s, cancel the embargo, and add one node to the persistent Trade Chain. Those fixed actions dispatch by `tv_trade_selected_good` into generated per-good script branches where needed, so the GUI does not instantiate per-good buttons. Each point of virtual demand or production locks 5% monopoly level, while each embargo locks 30%. Virtual demand uses generated `goods_demand` entries and is applied as `add_temporary_demand` with `months = -1`; set/increase/decrease/cancel actions remove and reapply it as needed, monthly monopoly maintenance removes it when the monopoly is lost, and the Trade League monthly fallback removes all virtual demand while the Grand Merchant cannot maintain monopolies. Virtual production tracks `tv_trade_virtual_supply_applied_<good>`, applies `add_goods_supply` once when active, removes it by applying a negative `add_goods_supply` amount before changing/canceling/suspending, and reapplies it when maintenance conditions recover. Embargo applies a 2-month `merchant_power_severe_penalty` through `add_merchant_power`. Action variables persist on the leader country when the monopoly is lost, but effects are applied only while `tv_trade_monopoly_<good> >= 1`. Every generated action is also registered in `tv_trade_league_list.txt` and `messagetypes.txt`. The Trade League overview shows a generated strength card with distance/flow/strength values and one dynamic `ShowModifierEffect(Concatenate('tv_trade_chain_strength_display_', ...))` row, followed by chain cards: the capital market card first, existing node cards in order, and one add-node card while count is below 20. The generated Trade League custom tab shows one rectangular slot card per unlocked monopoly slot, each with a progress piechart and centered good icon, good/stage labels, final percentage and origin/transit/consumer monopoly type, plus origin, transit network, and consumer percentage rows. Selecting a filled slot refreshes the existing projected detail card and leader-only fixed action card for the selected good; the detail card keeps representative origin/transit/consumer market tables for decision support.

Governor automation note: `tv_enable_governor_automation` / `tv_disable_governor_automation` toggle the country variable `tv_govhouse_governor_automation_enabled` from the overview `button_checkbox_auto` checkbutton beside `tv_auto_assign_governors`. When enabled, governor death, manual removal, location ownership changes, governor country moves, a governor becoming ruler, and marriage/royal-marriage cases where a governor becomes a consort each run one guarded `tv_govhouse_auto_assign_if_enabled_effect` pass; that helper delegates to `tv_governor_auto_assign_effect` only while a living Grand General is available, so the automation does not rely on monthly polling.

Governor assignment country note: new Local Governor assignments store `tv_governor_country` on the character so event-driven vacancy cleanup can find the original appointing country even if the character's current owner changes.

Governor autonomous task note: `tv_governor_select_yearly_task_effect` sets one active task per regional governor. The six tasks are restore prosperity, integrate region, convert culture, convert religion, strengthen recruitment, and strengthen order; integration is one of these autonomous tasks, not a separate always-on governor modifier. The task values scale from ADM/DIP/MIL, are displayed through `tv_govhouse_display_task_type/value`, and are refreshed into region-wide location modifiers by `tv_governor_apply_modifiers_effect`.

Regional Governor random events: `tv_govhouse_governor_events_monthly_pulse` runs through the generated `monthly_country_pulse` registry and only triggers if at least one living governor has an owned governed region. It uses a single monthly country-level roll with 180 no-event weight plus 20 event weights, giving a 10% total monthly chance. Events `tv_govhouse.100`-`.119` each pick one random eligible governor in `immediate`, save the governor, governed region, and a representative owned location as event scopes, then apply one-year region-wide location modifiers and/or immediate or one-year country effects. The 20-event set covers two-choice good events, one-option good events, two-choice tradeoff events, two-choice bad events, and one-option bad events.

Governor's House laws: the IO uses vanilla international-organization parliament law votes through `tv_govhouse_assembly`, and `tv_govhouse.gui` reuses the Diplomatic Alliance three-column parliament vote-card UI for support / not-voted / opposition rows. Only the locked `leader_country` / Supreme Overlord can initiate policy votes. The Supreme Overlord special status always contributes `special_status_power = 100`; each subject-chain member votes through `tv_govhouse_subject_status`, whose dynamic special-status power is based on its relative strength against the leader (`root.relative_strength(scope:recipient.leader_country) * 200`). The assembly keeps a leader-capital `parliament_seat` synchronized at creation and subject-chain refresh so non-HRE IO parliament law votes have a valid seat. Six generated core law groups (`tv_govhouse_expedition_goal_law`, `tv_govhouse_governor_appointment_law`, `tv_govhouse_local_autonomy_law`, `tv_govhouse_religious_mission_law`, `tv_govhouse_frontier_military_law`, `tv_govhouse_imperial_integration_law`) each have four effect-bearing country policies, and their generated `country_modifier` blocks are intentionally unfiltered so policy effects apply to every Governor's House member country. `tv_govhouse_grand_general_power_law` sits in the new `tv_govhouse_grand_general` category: Marching Orders gives newly appointed Grand Generals +10% initial loyalty, Garrison Orders makes governor task/function effects 20% stronger, and Supreme Command gives newly appointed Grand Generals -10% initial loyalty while doubling General Tactics and War Plan progress gain. Four generated Local Governor Function laws sit in `tv_govhouse_governor_functions`: the ADM/DIP/MIL function laws decide which scaled local modifiers governors apply, while `tv_govhouse_governor_duty_tendency_law` adds +0.30 task-selection weight to one of the six autonomous governor tasks. Every generated policy has a data-driven AI base vote bias from -60 to +60 in 20-point steps, favoring subject-friendly, traditional/conservative, and profit-seeking choices; when a policy is the current proposed law target (`ai_io_law_target`), the AI also adds opinion of the IO leader scaled from -200..+200 opinion to -100..+100 vote bias plus +5 vote bias per leader diplomatic reputation. All eleven laws keep `requires_vote = yes` and their generated `allow` block requires `uses_parliament_for_law_votes_trigger = yes`; the IO starts with no initial policies. There is no monthly default-law backfill.

Governor's House War Tent: requires an alive Grand General. The custom War Tent tab has three units: General Tactics, War Plan, and Main Actions. On war entry, `tv_govhouse_war_entry_effect` proposes one random tactic from the eleven-tactic pool and fires `tv_govhouse.40`; the player approves or rejects it. While continuously at war, the monthly pulse has a 5% chance to propose a replacement tactic, excluding the tactic already active. Approved tactics are applied as country-scoped static modifiers and are cleared when the country leaves all wars or loses its Grand General; this approximates general tactics because the army-wide combat modifiers did not apply reliably from the stored character path. Supreme Command doubles the applied tactic modifier size, including the Capital Raid location modifier. War plans are mutually exclusive: offensive plans select a target country and, while at war with that target, scale Siege Ability by progress x 0.2% and Discipline by progress x 0.1%; defensive plans need no target and, while in any defensive war, scale Fort Defense by progress x 0.2% and Military Tactics by progress x 0.005. On war entry, applicable offensive/defensive plan bonuses are activated through `tv_govhouse.41` / `tv_govhouse.42` events instead of being granted directly by the war-entry effect. Plan progress increases monthly by Grand General adm x 0.02 up to 100, doubled under Supreme Command; aborting/changing target resets it, and peace with an offensive plan target aborts that plan.

Governor's House header action note: `tv_govhouse.gui` adds a fixed-size semi-transparent Grand General Actions card to the IO header. It displays, in priority order, the appointed Grand General's commanded army link, wartime tactic planning, peacetime subject-annexation assistance, peacetime inspections, or the no-Grand-General fallback. The peacetime annexation row is driven by the country cache `tv_govhouse_annexing_subject_active`, refreshed by `tv_govhouse_update_general_action_display_effect` from the existing monthly War Tent path so the GUI does not scan subject diplomacy directly.

8. **Global Victory Leaderboard** — Monthly per-path ranking of the top 5 countries for each victory path. `tv_update_leaderboard_effect` writes situation variables `tv_<path>_rank_1_country` through `tv_<path>_rank_5_country`; the Victory Situation page for the selected path displays those countries with flag, country name, the selected path circle icon, that path's milestone count, and that path's score text.

Governor's House eligibility/death note: new governors and Grand Generals exclude female characters and artists at the selection/action-effect entry points. There is no monthly legacy-eligibility cleanup, to keep the IO monthly path focused on active governor bonus refresh. `tv_govhouse_governor_death` is registered under `on_character_death`, fires `tv_govhouse.1`, removes the dead governor from `tv_governor_list`, clears their region modifiers, and rebuilds the display lists immediately.

Grand General training note: `tv_train_grand_general` applies the one-year `tv_grand_general_training_modifier` country modifier defined in `src/in_game/common/static_modifiers/tv_govhouse_modifiers.txt` (`court_spending_efficiency = -0.10`), tracks `tv_gg_training_months` on the Grand General via monthly pulse, mirrors it to the country-side GUI display variable `tv_gg_training_progress`, resolves at 12 months through `tv_training_resolve_effect`, and routes to skill gain, trait-path upgrade, injury, or death events before clearing state.

Grand General death notification note: natural Grand General deaths trigger `tv_govhouse.2` from `tv_govhouse_char_death`; training-caused deaths set `tv_gg_training_death_notified` before `kill_character = this` so the already-shown training death event is not duplicated.

Governor's House balance-of-power backlash note: `tv_govhouse_power_balance` is an IO variable in the 0..1 range and is checked monthly by `tv_govhouse_negative_monthly_pulse` (registered through `data/pulse_registry.yaml`). `tv_govhouse_power_equilibrium` recalculates monthly from a -20% base, the Grand General's share of combined ruler/general abilities, `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation`, and a subtraction equal to the mirrored Grand General loyalty value; `tv_govhouse_power_balance` then moves toward that equilibrium by up to 0.6% per month. Below 25% no backlash events fire. At 25%/50%/75% the pulse uses a single `random_list` roll with a 300 total weight so at most one backlash event can appear per month, and each eligible event has weight 1/300. Events `tv_govhouse.10`-`.13` are tier 1, `.20`-`.25` are tier 2, and `.30`-`.34` are tier 3. Every backlash event has exactly two options: accept the negative effect, or pay an alternative resource and call `tv_govhouse_reduce_power_balance_effect` to lower the balance by 5 percentage points. Tier 2 adds rare art/artist losses; tier 3 upgrades the local satisfaction loss to an area-wide effect and adds a cabinet crisis. The Governor's House GUI shows right-aligned fixed-width 25/50/75/100% markers and four warning rows below the Balance of Power bar for the <25, 25-49, 50-74, and 75-100 segments.

Grand General replacement cost note: generated Remove/Change Grand General actions call `tv_govhouse_pay_grand_general_replacement_cost_effect` before resetting `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation`; the cost is Government Power equal to `tv_govhouse_power_consolidation * 20`, so a fully consolidated Grand General costs 20 government power to remove or replace.

## IO Architecture Design Decisions

The character-led TV IOs (`tv_arts_exhibition`, `tv_diplomatic_alliance`, `tv_academy_of_sciences`, `tv_govhouse`, `tv_engineering_department`, `tv_trade_league`) share invariants that must never be broken.

### 1. leader_country = founding country, locked at creation

`on_creation = { set_leader_country = scope:actor }` runs once when the IO is created. `leader_country` is not reassigned by normal gameplay afterward; the only exception is the manual recovery action `tv_reinitialize_mod`, exposed at the top of the Victory Situation panel, which recreates missing victory-path IOs where possible and reassigns joined TV IO instances back to the current country. It controls IO permissions, `is_leader_of_international_organization` trigger, and `leader_country.modifier:` chained access in `monthly_change` scripted values.

### 2. No elections — leader_change_trigger_type = none is mandatory

All TV character-led IO type definitions keep `leader_change_trigger_type = none` and `leader_change_method = none`. These prevent the engine from reassigning `leader_country` automatically. `international_organization_chooses_new_leader` is **globally banned** from all TV IO-related code — this effect triggers the vanilla election process. Any call to it violates the no-elections design and risks silently reassigning `leader_country` to a different country.

### 3. Great person characters are independent country variables, not the vanilla ruler

The character displayed in the IO header is stored as a country variable on the `leader_country`:
- Arts Exhibition: `tv_arts_exhibition_leader_char`
- Diplomatic Alliance: `tv_diplomatic_alliance_leader_char`
- Academy of Sciences: `tv_academy_leader_char`
- Governor's House: `tv_conquest_general_char`
- Engineering Department: `tv_great_engineer_char`
- Trade League: `tv_grand_merchant_char`

These are set/cleared exclusively by the generated generic actions in `tv_io_leader_actions.txt`. The vanilla `leader_country.GetGovernment.GetRuler` and `appointed_leader.*` are different concepts and must not be used in TV IO calculations.

### 4. IO variable monthly_change uses chained character attribute access

All IO variable `monthly_change` scripted values that depend on the appointed great person must use `leader_country.var:tv_xxx_leader_char.attribute` chained access — never `appointed_leader.attribute`. Examples:
- CORRECT: `value = leader_country.var:tv_academy_leader_char.adm`
- FORBIDDEN: `value = appointed_leader.adm`

### 5. IO monthly_effect blocks are forbidden

TV international_organization type definitions must not use `monthly_effect` blocks. IO monthly effects have severe performance costs even when hidden. Keep visible typed-variable monthly arithmetic in each variable's `monthly_change`; route maintenance, completion checks, and side-effect work through registered country monthly pulses or explicit lifecycle hooks.

### 6. Header display uses blockoverride

All character-led IO panels use `blockoverride` on their header template to display the appointed character (read from the country variable via `GetPlayer.MakeScope.GetVariable(...).GetCharacter`). Never use the vanilla `GetLeaderCountry.GetGovernment.GetRulerOrRegent` accessor chain.

### 7. All TV IOs have unique = no

Each country pursuing the relevant victory path creates its own independent IO instance. The `international_organization:type_name` scope link is **invalid** for non-unique types. Always use `every_international_organizations_member_of = { limit = { international_organization_type = international_organization_type:<io_type> } ... }`.

## AI Workflow Knowledge

The currently supported release localization set is English and Simplified Chinese. German localization remains future work: `scripts/validate.py` must not require German key completeness or static-modifier names merely because a partial German directory exists.

AI workflow state is maintained as project source, not by manual human follow-up. `scripts/ai_context.py` builds the task-scoped context from changed or explicit files, generated-file metadata, `docs/knowledge/anti_patterns.yaml`, short domain risk cards in `docs/knowledge/risk_cards/`, and content markers for `variable_map` / `global_variable_map` / `local_variable_map` usage. Current registered risk cards: `events.md`, `generic_actions.md`, `gui.md`, `international_organizations.md`, `on_action.md`, `wonders.md`, `philosophy_debate.md`, and `trade_league.md`; event files route to the events card because option tooltips can pre-evaluate option effect chains, including `hidden_effect`, while IO definitions, IO laws, and country interactions that find or mutate TV IOs route to the international-organization card because IO scopes are high risk. The `wonders`, `philosophy_debate`, and `trade_league` domains are not isolated in their own directory — their files are named-but-interspersed across `common/scripted_effects`, `static_modifiers`, `building_types`, `generic_actions`, and `gui` — so `ai_context.py` routes them by filename substring (`FILENAME_DOMAIN_RULES`) instead of path prefix: `wonder`/`engineering_department` filenames route to `wonders.md`, `philosophy_debate`/`world_debate`/`academy_debate` filenames route to `philosophy_debate.md`, and `trade_league`/`trade_chain` filenames route to `trade_league.md`. Files containing variable-map content route to the local Variable maps documentation and prioritize the `variable_map_scope_link_used_direct_rhs` anti-pattern because direct quoted map lookups are unsafe as RHS/effect parameters. Anti-pattern entries use `detectability` to distinguish safe regex lint (`lint`), parser/semantic rules (`needs_parser`), and AI-facing warnings (`advisory`), and may name a semantic `validator` implemented in `scripts/validate.py`. `scripts/validate.py` enforces safe lint rules, emits generic-action pre-evaluation and interaction-target chooser warnings, rejects visible generic-action calls to registered hidden-only cleanup helpers, emits IO policy AI-scope warnings/errors, fails duplicate main-menu localization keys within the same language pack, checks that every static modifier has `STATIC_MODIFIER_NAME_<id>` localization in every supported main-menu language, rejects dotted event references whose numeric event ID is `>= 10000`, rejects direct `effect = { ... }` child blocks inside event options, walks `monthly_country_pulse` registry callbacks and TV helper calls to require the configured one-day event delay, checks that risk cards remain registered in `ai_context.py`, checks that each TV IO type has a matching `main_menu/gfx/interface/icons/international_organizations/<io_type>.dds` GetIcon asset, rejects TV IO `monthly_effect` blocks, checks that generated main and M&T compatibility `location_window.gui` outputs match their current generators/reference inputs, and checks that TV does not stack a second direct `effect` onto hardcoded vanilla on_action hooks such as `on_annex` or `on_winning_war`. `scripts/test_lint_rules.py` runs fixture tests from `tests/fixtures/anti_patterns/<rule_id>/` for selected lint regexes and registered semantic validators.

All AI workflow command examples distinguish normal user terminals from managed sandboxes. In Codex, Claude subagents, or other managed sandboxes, project Python scripts are run with `C:\Users\Hades\anaconda3\envs\eu5\python.exe scripts\...`; agents must not run or retry `conda run -n eu5` there because the read-only Anaconda base metadata path can make it hang. The `conda run --no-capture-output -n eu5 python ...` form is reserved for normal user terminals and generated `Regen:` documentation lines. `scripts/ai_context.py` prints managed-sandbox validation suggestions with the direct interpreter form.

For Engineering Department wonders, remember the noncapital building rule: EU5 building `modifier` / `raw_modifier` are local, while `capital_country_modifier` only applies country effects if the building is in the capital. Since wonders can be outside the capital, final wonder buildings keep base national/global effects in their `modifier` block only as display data; the real base country effects are generated as level-gated Country Auto modifiers, while real ritual country effects are applied as static modifiers when the selected ritual completes.

Generated files that copy vanilla sources preserve the copied vanilla content verbatim except for explicit TV insertion points. `gen_character_title.py` and `gen_messagetypes.py` each write identical full copies to both mod roots, containing the complete TV title/message union. Their database winner order is not reliable even with a declared dependency, so a per-root subset is unsafe. `check_vanilla_copy_integrity()` validates the copied base, equality of the singleton outputs, and per-root generic-action message coverage. The on_action bridge registry generator emits one TV-owned bridge file, `tv_pulse_bridges.txt`, containing shared parent hooks such as `monthly_country_pulse`, `yearly_country_pulse`, `on_character_death`, `on_ruler_death`, startup/load hooks, war hooks, subject hooks, ownership hooks, institution-embraced hooks, capital-moved hooks, and CMF callbacks. Those blocks register only TV-owned callbacks through `on_actions = { ... }` and let vanilla or feature files provide the rest of each hook body. The bridge file must not use vanilla filenames such as `country_monthly.txt`, because those collide with vanilla files and can replace rather than extend them. Feature on_action files should keep only named `tv_*` callback bodies; parent hook bridge registration belongs in `data/pulse_registry.yaml` and generated `tv_pulse_bridges.txt`. The intended singleton insertions are TV character-title entries from `data/io_leaders.yaml` and per-root generic-action message types from `scripts/gen_messagetypes.py`; never hand-edit either root's generated full copy. `data/pulse_registry.yaml` owns the bridge on_action names and `settings.monthly_country_pulse_event_delay_days`, currently 1; any TV event whose conditions are checked by a `monthly_country_pulse` callback must use `days = 1` on direct `trigger_event_*` calls or `delay = { days = 1 }` before native `events` / `random_events` ids.

## Manual UI Notes

- `src_engineering_department/in_game/gui/location_window.gui` is generated from vanilla plus TV insertions. The location-scene wonder card uses fixed generated templates: three compact slots and five hover-tooltip slots, both filled by unique wonders first and generic wonders second, with one overflow line in the hover tooltip for any additional wonders beyond five. Slot state stores numeric ids, level, and ritual style only. Unique historical sites display non-starting wonders at level 0 before construction and game-start historical wonders at stored level 1 after startup/load initialization; level 0 tooltip rows show the name/image and `TV_LOCATION_WONDER_NO_EFFECT` instead of trying to route to a non-existent level-0 modifier or ritual effect. GUI names use dynamic clickable concept links built from id-based `tv_wonder_display_<id>` route concepts; compact-card images use cropped `tv_wonder_display_image_<id>` route concepts, tooltip illustrations use full 3:2 `tv_wonder_display_full_image_<id>` route concepts shared with Engineering Department overview previews, country/local effect columns use dynamic `ShowModifierEffect` keys backed by generated static mirrors and unreachable script references, and ritual effect lists are recovered through `tv_wonder_display_<id>_ritual_<style>_location_tooltip_effect` route names; both the ritual effect list and its mutually-exclusive "no effect" fallback (a plain `text_single`, not `TooltipTextBlock`) are sized to match the country/local modifier columns (13pt text, 22px rows) while keeping the full effect-column width. Each expanded tooltip row also opens with an untitled requirements block above the country/local modifier columns (no separate title text, since the location the panel is already anchored to makes the "construction conditions" framing self-evident), showing the location's own base site rule requirements via `ShowTriggerConditionsForScope` against a generated `tv_wonder_display_<id>_base_site_rules_trigger` alias, evaluated directly in the location's own scope (no `any_owned_location` wrapper, since the GUI root is already the location). That alias re-embeds the wonder's site-rule condition list directly rather than delegating to the real gameplay trigger `tv_wonder_location_meets_<key>_base_site_rules_trigger`, so unique wonders' `this = location:<site>` guard (still required on the real trigger for construction eligibility) is omitted from the display — the tooltip is already showing that exact location, so the row would be redundant. Its overview tab contains Trade League invitation/expulsion controls plus the Trade Chain UI: a strength card with aligned length/flow/strength values, weak/medium/strong tier label, and one dynamic `ShowModifierEffect(Concatenate('tv_trade_chain_strength_display_', ...))` display row, followed by ordered market cards with slot 1 fixed to the capital market and one add-node card while count is below 20. Chain cards use 72px piechart/add-circle left glyphs, market owner / own capacity / trade advantage / trade profit columns, 8px spacing, and generated progress-bar backgrounds aligned to the pie/add centers. The vanilla Laws tab is available as Trade Laws for the five three-level Trade League law groups. The Resolutions tab is Commercial Intelligence: once a Grand Merchant is appointed, all members can page through fixed projected rows for global markets, league total trade advantage, and network strength; selecting a market opens a detail card with the top three merchant-power countries, network strength, a 0-100 progress bar, and leader-only Build/Cancel Network controls. Intelligence network state is stored on the Trade League leader by market slot; the active market gains Grand Merchant DIP / 100 strength each month, inactive markets lose 1 strength, and each full strength point is synchronized as keyed permanent `tv_trade_intelligence_network` merchant power for current members in that market. The custom tab is Monopolies: it remains locked until a Grand Merchant is appointed, then shows one rectangular card per unlocked monopoly slot, with 1 slot by default and 2 slots at 10 members. Slot actions refresh the player's slot and `tv_trade_selected_*` projection variables from the leader-owned monopoly state, so members can browse independently. Selecting a filled slot opens one projected monopoly detail card and one leader-only fixed action card for virtual demand, virtual production, and embargo controls.
